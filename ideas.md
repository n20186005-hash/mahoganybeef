# Mahogany Beef Market & Bulalohan — design directions

## Three distinct approaches

| Theme Name | Very Brief Intro | Probability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ridge Market Ledger | A warm, tactile market journal that turns a practical food stop into a distinctly Tagaytay planning companion. It balances useful route details with the visual honesty of a public market. | 0.07 |
| Taal Mist Postcard | An airy travel postcard built around highland calm, lake air and quiet sightlines. Its softer pacing risks underplaying the place’s lively food-market character. | 0.03 |
| Night Bulalo Signage | A high-contrast, late-evening food-stall identity shaped by painted menus and sodium lamps. It is energetic but conflicts with the more useful daytime market-planning brief. | 0.09 |

## Chosen approach: Ridge Market Ledger

### Design Movement

The site will use an **editorial travel field-journal** aesthetic, borrowing its sense of materiality from Filipino market signage, letterpress menus and well-used recipe notebooks. The experience should feel observant rather than polished into a generic tourism brochure.

### Core Principles

The page will give real market photographs room to breathe, layer practical information in high-contrast “ledger” panels, and use asymmetry to suggest a walking route through the market. Every decorative device must clarify a moment of planning or add a sense of place. Large image crops will always include an overlay or an adjacent solid background so text remains legible.

### Color Philosophy

**Mahogany Brown** is the signature anchor, evoking the avenue name, wooden food stalls and the seriousness of a simmering broth. It will be tempered by rice-paper cream for readable, sunlit negative space and an avocado-green accent drawn from Tagaytay produce. The colors are earthy, local and warm rather than tropical-cliché.

### Layout Paradigm

The page will read as a **folded market itinerary**, not as a centered card stack. A wide editorial hero begins with a content “ticket” set to one side of real photography. Subsequent sections alternate between full-bleed photo strips, off-axis information columns and concise route markers. On mobile, that order collapses into a linear, highly legible walk-through.

### Signature Elements

The identity repeats three restrained motifs: a steam-and-leaf brand mark, thin contour-like route lines and small stamped labels for visitor planning data. Decorative assets are original graphics; all contextual photographs are real images with clearly linked source attribution.

### Interaction Philosophy

Navigation should function like a practical market map: terse labels, clear anchors and an always-available “Plan your visit” route. Hover states are tactile—slight lifts, underlines and stamp-color shifts—rather than flashy effects.

### Animation

Photography and ledger panels enter with a short 180–240ms opacity-and-translate reveal when they scroll into view, respecting reduced-motion preferences. Buttons compress slightly on press; no animated layout changes, auto-playing media or attention-seeking loops will be used.

### Typography System

**DM Serif Display** gives headings a food-journal authority, while **Manrope** provides compact, highly readable operational text. Section labels use uppercase Manrope with measured tracking; large headings remain left-aligned and may use italic emphasis only for edible or geographic sensory words.

### Brand Essence

**An independent field guide for travelers who want Tagaytay’s market-born bulalo experience without the tourist gloss.** The personality is grounded, observant and appetizing.

### Brand Voice

Headlines should be direct and sensory, never generic. CTAs should sound like practical invitations. Example lines: “Come for the simmering broth. Leave with a market story.” and “Find the market, then follow the steam.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The logo combines a simplified steaming bowl, a mahogany leaf and a market-awning arc in a single carved symbol. The wordmark uses a deliberately spaced serif treatment rather than a default UI font. The favicon is the same bowl-and-leaf mark reduced to its strongest silhouette.

### Signature Brand Color

**Mahogany Brown — #4A2419** is the owned color that appears in the logo, section labels, selected navigation state and key calls to action.

## Style Decisions

The design will avoid centered hero copy, purple gradients, uniform rounded rectangles and generic restaurant clichés. The prominent hero image must be a real market photograph with an explicit dark overlay; generated brand graphics will remain secondary and decorative.
